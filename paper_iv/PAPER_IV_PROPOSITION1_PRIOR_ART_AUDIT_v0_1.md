## 1. Proposition under audit

Current manuscript statement, in substance:

> A scoped PASS/FAIL result for a subset of material branches of a theory family does not entail a family-level sufficiency/exclusion statement if a material branch remains unresolved or if a residual required by the family-level claim is undefined because the same-realization/common-domain map is incomplete.

Corollary:

> Reducing numerical uncertainty in an adjacent but non-composable calculation cannot by itself make a structurally undefined family-level residual defined.

## 2. Neighboring bodies of prior art

### A. Composite/model-family hypothesis testing

Standard statistical model comparison treats a model or theory family as a defined hypothesis class. A conclusion about one parameter point or one submodel is not automatically a conclusion about the entire composite class. Bayesian model comparison likewise requires a specified model/likelihood and parameter domain before evidence is defined.

Implication for Paper IV: the quantifier logic underlying the branch non-promotion part of Proposition 1 is not claimed as a new theorem of statistics or logic.

### B. Partial identification

The statistical/econometric literature explicitly studies partially identified models, where data and assumptions do not identify a unique parameter or model object. P. C. B. Phillips, “Partially Identified Econometric Models,” *Econometric Theory* 5 (1989) 181–240, is a clear neighboring reference: it studies inference when conventional identification conditions fail.

Implication for Paper IV: the statement that precision cannot recover a parameter/object that is structurally unidentified has established analogues. KMQGB's contribution is the physical realization/comparator implementation, not invention of the generic concept of identification failure.

### C. Gravitational-theory model selection

The gravitational-wave and modified-gravity literature uses Bayesian model/theory comparison between specified waveform/template families. Such analyses presuppose that the candidate theory produces a defined likelihood/template in the same data domain. Reviews of GR tests make this explicit when describing separate GR and alternative-theory model families and Bayesian evidence.

Implication for Paper IV: common-domain model comparison is established practice once model predictions are comparator-ready. Paper IV focuses one logical layer earlier: whether a cross-regime or cross-source quantum-gravity object is sufficiently specified to enter such comparison at all.

### D. Comparative quantum-gravity literature

Existing QG reviews and maps compare approaches by theoretical programme, phenomenological signature, experimental channel, or possible relationships among frameworks. Representative neighbors already in the article include Barrau (2017), Mielczarek & Trześniewski (2018), Addazi et al. (2022), the *Handbook of Quantum Gravity* (2024), and the 2025 multi-messenger QG phenomenology roadmap.

The first-pass search did not identify a directly matching published protocol that simultaneously:

1. freezes a cross-framework judge before family outcomes are interpreted;
2. requires explicit same-realization provenance before adjacent literature objects are composed;
3. distinguishes child/subfamily scope from parent-family scope by an explicit promotion gate;
4. preserves missing transport/normalization/comparator objects as non-exclusion states; and
5. executes those rules across a declared multi-family quantum-gravity census.

This is not sufficient for a priority claim; a deeper audit is still required.

## 3. Novelty decision

### Not novel as a bare logical theorem

The bare implication in Proposition 1 follows from ordinary quantifier logic plus identification/model-definition requirements. It should **not** be advertised as a new theorem of statistics, philosophy of science, or mathematical logic.

### Potentially novel as an operational quantum-gravity protocol result

The defensible contribution is the explicit embedding of that logic into a frozen physical-comparison architecture with:

- a realization vector carrying parent theory, state, boundary, gauge/regulator, truncation, signature, normalization, scale and source routing;
- a common-domain residual that is undefined unless the mapping object exists;
- a family-level promotion rule;
- an executed fourteen-family census;
- machine-preserved `BLOCKED != FAIL` semantics;
- prospective stress tests in which new external results change the evidence state without changing the judge.

Thus the article should call Proposition 1 a **protocol-level proposition** or **formal consequence of the frozen comparison semantics**, not a fundamental theorem.
