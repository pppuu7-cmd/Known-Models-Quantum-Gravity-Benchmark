## Fixed response object

For Poisson sprinklings into `1+1` Minkowski spacetime, Hinrichsen–Kastrati construct a retarded scalar propagator directly from the causal-set link matrix `L`. The averaged massless object is associated with a normalized matrix exponential `exp(L)`. The massive response is generated by the standard scattering series and agrees well with the continuum retarded propagator after sprinkling average. The inverse kernel `exp(-L)` is proposed as a candidate discrete d'Alembertian.

Kastrati then keeps the link prescription fixed and changes the embedding to conformally flat constant-curvature `AdS_1+1` and `dS_1+1`. Although causal order is unchanged, link probabilities depend on causal-diamond volume. The leading large-distance correction maps to a continuum scalar response with

`m_eff^2 = xi_eff R`,  with  `xi_eff = 1/6`.

The curvature coupling is therefore an emergent response of the same microscopic link rule, not a parameter inserted manually into the continuum equation. Numerical sprinkling studies support the analytic curvature response and indicate persistence as sprinkling density is increased.

## Why this cannot close the causal-set family

The object above is QFT on causal sets generated by sprinkling into an already specified continuum background. It does not supply the missing inverse direction required by fundamental quantum gravity:

`fundamental causal-set dynamics/measure -> dynamically selected manifoldlike ensembles -> emergent 3+1 Lorentzian geometry -> normalized gravitational observable`.

In particular:

- the background manifold is supplied to the sprinkling construction rather than dynamically recovered from the quantum measure;
- the demonstrated propagation/curvature response is `1+1`, not a `3+1` gravitational-sector observable;
- the scalar propagator is not a graviton/spacetime observable and cannot disposition the family residual;
- suppression of broad layered/non-manifoldlike classes by the BDG path integral does not prove that the surviving measure is dominated by the desired manifoldlike `3+1` sector;
- Carlip explicitly notes that the remaining unsuppressed causal sets are not sufficiently characterized and that the BDG action itself is not derived from a first-principles fundamental dynamics;
- the Royal Society 2026 programme still identifies dynamics, universality, observables and classical-limit/emergence as central open nonperturbative questions;
- the 2026 quantum-counting algorithm for the BDG action improves evaluation complexity but does not by itself define or solve the physical quantum measure.
