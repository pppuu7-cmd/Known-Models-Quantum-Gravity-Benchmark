## Abstract — draft v0.1

Quantum-gravity programmes are commonly compared across incompatible levels of description: different states, signatures, regulators, truncations, normalizations, observables, response orders, and low-energy limits are often placed side by side without an explicit proof that they represent the same physical realization or the same comparator domain. This makes it difficult to distinguish genuine scientific tension from missing translation objects. We present the Known Models / Quantum Gravity Benchmark (KMQGB), an application layer built under the frozen Relativity–Quantum Interface Reconstruction (RQIR) Core v1.0. The benchmark maps framework-native objects into a common observable/comparator language while forbidding model-dependent retuning of the judge, enforcing same-realization provenance, and preserving scientifically legitimate BLOCKED states when required theory, observable, normalization, transport, or comparator objects are unavailable. A predeclared Tier-1 census contains 14 major framework families, with all Tier-2 classification entries resolved. The operational proving ground and D7 decision infrastructure are complete and validated, but strict family-level scientific closure is not: only the GR/EFT baseline is presently terminal, whereas 13 candidate families remain partial or blocked under the frozen family-scope contract. Consequently, the global D7 classifier is not authorized to return EXISTING_SUFFICIENT, ADAPT_EXISTING, HYBRID_REQUIRED, or NEW_REQUIRED. The central result is therefore not an exclusion census but an auditable failure taxonomy for cross-framework comparison: the protocol localizes whether a comparison is limited by framework coverage, same-realization object completeness, common-domain comparability, or genuinely defined residual evidence. Case studies from asymptotic safety, loop quantum gravity/spinfoams, causal fermion systems, Hořava–Lifshitz gravity, and higher-derivative quantum gravity illustrate how apparently comparable predictions can remain nonterminal once provenance and comparator closure are enforced. The resulting benchmark establishes a reproducible route from heterogeneous quantum-gravity programmes to a future global decision without converting missing information into falsification.

## 1. Introduction — draft v0.1

The search for a quantum theory of gravity has produced a diverse landscape of frameworks rather than a single common model class. These programmes differ not only in microscopic assumptions, but also in the physical objects they calculate, the regimes in which those objects are defined, and the maps required to connect them to low-energy gravitational observables. As a result, a statement that two approaches make different predictions is not meaningful until the predictions have been transported into the same physical realization and the same observable/comparator domain.

A recurring problem is that missing theory-to-observable maps are easily confused with negative evidence. A framework may possess a well-defined ultraviolet construction but lack a controlled same-realization path to an infrared observable. Another may provide a semiclassical observable but not a normalized comparator at the same response order. Yet another may contain several materially distinct quantization branches for which a result in one branch cannot be promoted to a family-level verdict. Treating such cases as ordinary failures would artificially strengthen any conclusion that existing quantum-gravity approaches are insufficient.

RQIR was designed to separate these logically distinct questions. In Papers I–III, the reconstruction programme progressively freezes calibration survival, statistical identifiability, and resource/observable closure before a model is allowed to support a physical claim. Paper IV applies that discipline to the landscape of known quantum-gravity frameworks. The essential methodological choice is that the judge is frozen before the framework census is interpreted: framework-specific adapters may translate native objects into RQIR observables, but a difficult or unfavorable model result cannot modify the benchmark semantics.

The resulting Known Models / Quantum Gravity Benchmark (KMQGB) is therefore not an attempt to rank research schools by reputation or to force heterogeneous theories into a single microscopic formalism. It is a controlled comparison layer. Its basic pipeline is

`framework-native object -> KMQGB adapter -> frozen RQIR observable/comparator -> terminal or BLOCKED status`.

This architecture supports three separations that are central to the present paper. First, repository and methodology completion are separated from scientific closure. Second, a scoped child result is separated from a verdict on an entire framework family. Third, absence of a required same-realization object is separated from a defined scientific residual. These distinctions make it possible to reach operational saturation without claiming that the physical decision problem has already been solved.

At the current benchmark state, the operational proving ground is complete and all internally actionable closure fronts have been exhausted under the declared rules. The Tier-1 census contains 14 major framework families and Tier-2 classification contains no unresolved entries. Nevertheless, strict terminal family coverage remains only 1/14, with the remaining 13 families parked behind explicit external theory, data, provenance, or reduction-map reopen conditions. The global D7 classifier therefore remains scientifically unauthorized. This is not a failure of the benchmark. It is one of its principal outputs: the method identifies exactly which missing objects prevent a stronger conclusion and forbids those absences from being silently promoted into evidence for a new theory.

This paper develops that result in four stages. We first define the frozen-core governance and major-framework coverage contract. We then introduce the D1–D7 decision gates and the evidence taxonomy used to distinguish terminal, partial, blocked, and scoped results. We next present the cross-framework benchmark matrix and detailed representative case studies. Finally, we show how the unresolved family-level objects propagate into the global D7 decision and define the precise conditions under which an existing framework, an adaptation, a hybrid construction, or a genuinely new candidate theory could later become scientifically authorized.

## 6. Representative case studies

### 6.1 Asymptotic Safety

Article role: demonstrate the distinction between programme-level Lorentzian gravitational contact evidence and a stable reproducible same-realization amplitude/comparator certificate.

Current scientific status: open reproducibility/composition object; not a family exclusion.

### 6.2 Loop Quantum Gravity / EPRL-spinfoam

Article role: show how internally derived identifiability and RG relations can constrain a valid bridge without proving that adjacent microscopic and effective descriptions already belong to one physical realization.

Current KMQGB relations include

`q = 1/gamma - gamma - Delta_gamma`,

`beta_Delta = beta_rho - (1 + 1/gamma^2) beta_gamma`,

`gamma = -cot(4 psi)`,

`Delta_gamma = 2 cot(8 psi) - q`,

and

`beta_Delta = -16 csc^2(8 psi) beta_psi - beta_q`.

The open object is the same-realization EPRL/spinfoam -> area-metric continuum/RG -> gamma_EFT chain with same-parent transport authority.

### 6.3 Causal Fermion Systems

Article role: demonstrate a framework with continuum/gravity structure and a correction-generation route but without the first explicit normalized beyond-continuum gravity correction tensor/coefficient and same-domain comparator required for a terminal row.

### 6.4 Hořava–Lifshitz gravity

Article role: demonstrate why materially distinct projectable and non-projectable/BPS branches cannot inherit one another's status. A UV result and a low-energy phenomenological result remain non-composable until the required same-realization trajectory and scalar/tensor normalization bridge are established.

### 6.5 Higher-derivative quantum gravity

Article role: show prescription/quantization-branch dependence. A scoped result in a fakeon or principal-value branch cannot be promoted to the entire higher-derivative family without terminal disposition of materially distinct branches and a fixed-branch normalized observable/comparator package.

1. A frozen observable/comparator judge can be applied across heterogeneous quantum-gravity programmes without changing its semantics to fit individual frameworks.

## 11. Conclusion — draft v0.1

We have constructed a frozen-core benchmark for comparing heterogeneous quantum-gravity frameworks at the level of explicit physical observables, realization provenance, and comparator closure. The present benchmark is operationally saturated but scientifically nonterminal: the methodology and D7 infrastructure are complete, while only one of fourteen Tier-1 family rows is presently terminal under the strict family-scope contract. This separation is itself a substantive result. It prevents missing cross-regime maps, normalization objects, or comparator definitions from being misreported as evidence against an entire research programme. The current benchmark therefore does not choose among an existing theory, an adaptation, a hybrid construction, or a new theory. Instead, it provides an auditable set of proof obligations whose eventual closure can make that decision scientifically meaningful.
