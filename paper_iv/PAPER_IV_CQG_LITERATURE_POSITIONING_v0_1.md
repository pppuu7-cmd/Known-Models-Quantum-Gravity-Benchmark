# RQIR Paper IV — CQG literature positioning v0.1

## Purpose

Define the novelty claim against existing comparative quantum-gravity literature before the Introduction is frozen for submission.

## Relevant neighboring literature

### Barrau (2017)

`Testing different approaches to quantum gravity with cosmology: An overview` compares several major quantum-gravity programmes through cosmological testing opportunities. It is an approach-oriented phenomenology overview, not a frozen cross-framework observable/comparator decision protocol.

### Mielczarek and Trześniewski (2018)

`Towards the map of quantum gravity` discusses possible links among several quantum-gravity approaches, including LQG, CDT, Hořava–Lifshitz gravity, asymptotic safety and Quantum Graphity. This is important prior art against any claim that Paper IV is the first work to map relationships among quantum-gravity programmes.

### Addazi et al. (2022)

`Quantum gravity phenomenology at the dawn of the multi-messenger era—A review` organizes quantum-gravity phenomenology around measurable signatures and multi-messenger probes. It establishes the importance of observable-facing quantum-gravity tests but does not impose the KMQGB family-scope/same-realization closure rule.

### Bambi, Modesto and Shapiro (eds.) (2024)

The `Handbook of Quantum Gravity` provides a broad modern taxonomy of major approaches and is the principal coverage reference for the Paper-IV Tier-1 census. It is a reference work, not a common-domain decision benchmark.

### Alves Batista et al. (2025), Classical and Quantum Gravity

`White paper and roadmap for quantum gravity phenomenology in the multi-messenger era` is especially important for CQG positioning. It surveys experimental/phenomenological channels and future requirements for quantum-gravity searches. Paper IV should present itself as complementary: KMQGB asks whether the theory object attached to an observable is sufficiently specified, transported and normalized to support cross-framework inference before experimental discrimination is attempted.

### Xu (2026), arXiv:2608.23117

`Relational Quantum Causal Processes toward Quantum Gravity with Controlled Einstein Response` is a recent conceptual neighbor. It constructs a controlled same-family benchmark in one explicit quantum-causal model and emphasizes common-regulator/fixed-band closure and non-fitted gravitational response. This weakens any broad novelty statement such as 'the first controlled benchmark connecting quantum and gravitational observables.' Paper IV is differentiated by executing a frozen family-scope comparison across a declared multi-framework census rather than constructing one same-family microscopic model.

## Safe novelty claim

Do not claim that Paper IV is the first comparison, map, benchmark or observable-based study of quantum-gravity approaches.

The defensible novelty claim is narrower:

> Paper IV introduces and executes a frozen cross-framework decision protocol in which major quantum-gravity families are evaluated only after framework-native objects are mapped into a common observable/comparator domain, adjacent results satisfy explicit same-realization provenance, and missing physical objects are quarantined as non-exclusion states. The contribution is the resulting family-level decision topology across a declared 14-family census, not a new catalogue of approaches.

## Strong CQG contrast sentence for the Introduction

Existing reviews and roadmaps largely organize the quantum-gravity landscape by theoretical programme, phenomenological signature or experimental channel. The present work addresses a different question: under what conditions do results from different quantum-gravity programmes constitute physically comparable statements about the same gravitational observable?

## Claims to avoid

- `first systematic comparison of quantum-gravity approaches`;
- `first benchmark of quantum gravity`;
- `first observable-based comparison`;
- `all major approaches have been tested` if 'tested' could be read as observational falsification;
- `13/14 theories fail`;
- `new quantum gravity is required` at the current D7 state.

## Current literature-search conclusion

The literature found so far contains broad framework maps, cosmological comparisons, experimental/phenomenological roadmaps and at least one recent same-family controlled benchmark. No directly matching multi-family frozen same-realization/common-comparator decision protocol has yet been identified in this search, but the manuscript should still avoid priority language until a deeper reference audit is complete.
