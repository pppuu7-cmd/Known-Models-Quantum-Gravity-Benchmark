## 1. Complex-analysis theorem

Let `f(z)` be entire and suppose that for sufficiently large `|z|`

`|f(z)| <= C (1+|z|)^N`

for finite constants `C,N`.

Cauchy estimates imply that every Taylor coefficient above order `N` vanishes. Hence `f` is a polynomial of degree at most `N`.

This is an exact complex-analysis statement.

## 4. Gravity-specific nuance

Polynomial boundedness of a gravitational S-matrix is not treated here as an unconditional theorem in every regime. Gravity is massless and strong-gravity behavior complicates standard massive-QFT arguments.

- **if** the candidate claims the ordinary polynomially bounded/local analytic S-matrix class, its pole-free entire correction collapses to a polynomial;
- **if not**, it must explicitly declare the weaker growth/nonlocality class and survive the E1 comparator/causality obligations.

This avoids both overclaiming a universal gravity theorem and hiding nonlocality behind the word `entire`.
