**Status:** constructive Q2 branch audit / A2 blocked unless a microscopic binding rule fixes the internal wavefunction.

**Scope:** attempts to obtain a common-clock massless spin-2 mode as a correlated composite of several Weyl-QCA constituents.

## 1. Motivation from the Maxwell-QCA positive control

Bisio, D'Ariano and Perinotti construct the photon as a correlated pair of independent Weyl-QCA Fermions.

Their bilinear operator has the schematic form

`F^mu(k) = integral dq f_k(q) phi^T(k/2-q) sigma^mu psi(k/2+q)`,

with

`integral dq |f_k(q)|^2 = 1`.

In the narrow-relative-momentum regime the pair evolves with the effective rotation generated by the Weyl symbol evaluated near `k/2`, and the relativistic limit reproduces Maxwell propagation. The internal function `f_k(q)` also controls composite-Boson properties and subleading corrections.

This is an important QCA control because momentum sharing solves the naive representation-lift speed problem without taking a fractional lattice root.

Reference: A. Bisio, G. M. D'Ariano, P. Perinotti, *Quantum cellular automaton theory of light*, Annals of Physics 368 (2016) 177-190, DOI 10.1016/j.aop.2016.02.009.

## 2. Spin-2 momentum-sharing idea

A schematic spin-2 analogue can use four Weyl constituents with total momentum `k`:

`p_a = k/4 + q_a`,

`sum_a q_a = 0`.

For a narrow distribution around small relative momenta,

`sum_a omega(p_a) = 4 omega(k/4) + relative-momentum corrections`.

Since the BCC Weyl dispersion is linear in the IR,

`4 omega(k/4) = omega(k) + higher lattice corrections`.

Thus a four-constituent composite can **kinematically** evade the factor-four same-clock failure of the naive `Sym^4[A(k)]` representation lift.

This is only a kinematic observation; it does not establish a graviton, bosonic statistics, universal coupling, or interacting gravity.

## 3. Generic composite operator

The most general fixed-total-momentum four-constituent spin-2 operator has the schematic form

`G_M(k) = integral dQ f_{k,M}(Q) C_M^{a1 a2 a3 a4} Psi_1,a1(p_1) Psi_2,a2(p_2) Psi_3,a3(p_3) Psi_4,a4(p_4)`,

where

- `Q` denotes three independent relative momenta after `sum q_a=0`;
- `C_M` projects/splices spin/flavor indices into a spin-2 ambient component `M`;
- `f_{k,M}(Q)` is the internal momentum/binding wavefunction;
- distinct Fermionic species/flavor or another antisymmetry mechanism may be required to realize the desired symmetric spin sector without Pauli annihilation.

Nothing in total momentum sharing alone fixes `f_{k,M}(Q)`.

## 4. A2 functional-freedom diagnosis

If `f_{k,M}(Q)` is chosen by hand subject only to normalization and narrow-support conditions, the proposed graviton contains an infinite-dimensional functional degree of freedom.

That freedom controls, in general,

- relative-momentum corrections to the dispersion;
- polarization leakage / non-TT admixture;
- effective composite commutation relations;
- finite-density saturation;
- form factors and residues;
- hard scattering and inelastic thresholds;
- coupling of the composite to matter and to itself.

Therefore the branch is classified

`A2_BLOCKED__UNFIXED_COMPOSITE_RELATIVE_WAVEFUNCTION`.

This is exactly the type of hidden freedom prohibited by `protocol/P4_FUNCTIONAL_FREEDOM_NO_GO.md`.

## 5. Why equal sharing is not a valid selector by itself

Setting

`q_a=0` for all constituents

or choosing a delta-function relative wavefunction after observing the desired light cone is not a microscopic derivation.

In position space an exact relative-momentum delta corresponds to a delocalized relative coordinate and cannot be used as a strictly local bound-state rule without additional dynamics.

Similarly, choosing a narrow Gaussian width by hand introduces a new scale/function and leaves hard observables dependent on the arbitrary choice.

Thus

`desired common clock -> choose f`

is post-hoc fitting, not P4 selection.

## 6. What would rescue the branch

A composite spin-2 route can re-enter A2 only if one finite microscopic parent dynamically fixes the internal state.

Acceptable examples in principle include

- a local finite-depth interaction whose isolated bound-state eigenvector uniquely determines `f_k(Q)` up to a finite set of parent parameters;
- a finite local gauge/constraint code whose physical one-particle spin-2 excitation has a uniquely fixed internal momentum structure;
- an exact transfer matrix whose isolated helicity-2 pole/residue determines the bound-state wavefunction and shares one microscopic clock with matter.

The key condition is

`f_k(Q) = f_k(Q; theta)`

with finite fixed `theta`, rather than `f_k` being independent parent data.

## 7. Microscopic freedom test for a rescued composite

For any proposed binding update `U_bind(theta)`, KMQGB must verify

1. finite local Hilbert/register data;
2. finite interaction range/depth;
3. exact unitarity/constraint preservation;
4. isolated two-helicity composite branch;
5. finite-dimensional `theta` independent of hard order;
6. internal wavefunction derived from the same update;
7. common IR light cone with matter;
8. cubic universal coupling from the same parent;
9. quartic/higher response derived without independent composite form factors.

If the number of independent internal-shape parameters grows with basis resolution or hard order, the branch remains `FUNCTIONAL_FREEDOM_BLOCKED`.

Therefore even a dynamically fixed composite spin-2 state would still need A3/P5 profiling before any KG novelty claim.

## 9. Q2 conclusion

Three lowest-complexity free constructions are now separated:

1. `Sym^4` same group element: finite-range and spin-2, but common-clock speed fails by factor four;
2. fourth-root repair: common-clock phase repaired, but exact same-lattice finite range fails;
3. momentum-sharing composite: common-clock kinematics can work, but an arbitrary internal relative wavefunction creates A2 functional freedom unless a microscopic binding rule fixes it.

Thus the next meaningful branch is no longer a free kinematic trick.

It is a finite local **binding/constraint dynamics** problem, which is already the entrance to MISP2 Q3.
