## 1. Comparator architecture

Constructive gravity / gravitational closure starts from a prescribed matter action

`S_matter[A,G]`

on a tensorial background geometry `G` satisfying physical predictivity/energy conditions. The matter equations determine the admissible causal/initial-data structure. Requiring the geometric degrees of freedom to possess compatible canonical evolution on the same initial-data hypersurfaces yields a system of gravitational closure equations whose solutions provide admissible diffeomorphism-invariant gravitational actions.

Schematically,

`matter action + causal/predictive consistency -> closure equations -> gravitational action family`.

For ordinary Standard-Model-type matter on Lorentzian metric geometry, the established positive control recovers the Einstein-Hilbert family, leaving the gravitational and cosmological constants to experiment.

## 2. Why this is highly relevant after Iter138

Iter138 established a GR rigidity ceiling from purely gravitational/local consistency assumptions.

Constructive gravity supplies a different selection route:

`matter first -> gravity second`.

This proves that a complete gravitational action can, in principle, be selected by **cross-sector causal consistency** rather than by choosing a gravity operator directly.

Therefore a future proposal of the form

> derive the gravitational operator uniquely from the causal principal polynomial / predictivity structure of matter

is not architecture-new by itself.

## 3. Conditional nature of the selector

The closure mechanism requires the matter theory as input. Different admissible matter actions/geometries can generate different gravitational closure equations and different gravitational dynamics.

Hence the mechanism answers

`given matter dynamics, what gravity is compatible?`

rather than

`what finite physical law selects both matter and gravity from no arbitrary dynamical input?`

For KMQGB this means that the matter action, its field content and its lower-order interaction data are **parent data** unless they are independently derived.

A candidate cannot claim a complete P4 origin law if it merely chooses a novel matter sector and then runs gravitational closure.

## 4. Residual constants and finite freedom

Even strong closure need not produce a parameter-free theory. In the Standard-Model/metric control, the Einstein-Hilbert family retains at least the gravitational and cosmological constants as integration/physical constants.

In more general matter geometries the solution space can contain additional constants, and practical closure equations may be difficult to solve.

This is compatible with KMQGB finite-freedom requirements, but the surviving constants must be declared and shared prospectively rather than fitted separately in each observable block.
