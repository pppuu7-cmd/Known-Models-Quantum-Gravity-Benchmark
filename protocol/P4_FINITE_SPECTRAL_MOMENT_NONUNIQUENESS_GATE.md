## 1. Setup

Let a positive-frequency spectral measure be `dmu(omega) >= 0`, with moments

`mu_k = integral omega^k dmu(omega)`.

Suppose a proposed gravity parent supplies only finitely many moment/sum-rule constraints

`mu_0, ..., mu_D`.

The question is whether positivity + those moments uniquely determine `dmu`.

## 2. Exact finite witness

Take support

`omega = (1,2,3,4,5)`

and the fourth finite-difference vector

`v = (1,-4,6,-4,1)`.

For every polynomial of degree `k < 4`,

`sum_i v_i omega_i^k = 0`.

Start from positive base weights `(10,10,10,10,10)` and define

`w_plus  = (11,6,16,6,11)`,

`w_minus = (9,14,4,14,9)`.

Both measures are strictly positive and distinct, yet they have identical moments for `k=0,1,2,3`. Their fourth moments differ.

Therefore a finite positive moment set does not uniquely fix the spectral measure even in a tiny discrete example.

The executable reference verifies this exactly using rational arithmetic.

## 3. General mechanism

For `N` support points and only `D+1 < N` moment constraints, the Vandermonde moment map has a nontrivial nullspace. A sufficiently small perturbation along a null vector preserves positivity while leaving the constrained moments unchanged.

Thus finite moments generally carve out a convex family of positive spectral measures rather than a single envelope.

## 4. KMS does not repair the ambiguity

KMS relates positive- and negative-frequency thermal components once the positive-frequency spectral density is specified. If two distinct positive-frequency measures have the same finite moments, their KMS completions remain distinct thermal correlators while satisfying the same detailed-balance relation.

Hence

`finite moments + positivity + KMS != unique spectral function`.
