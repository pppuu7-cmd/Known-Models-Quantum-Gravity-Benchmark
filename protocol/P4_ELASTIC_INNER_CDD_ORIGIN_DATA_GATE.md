## 1. Scope and gravitational caveat

Consider a scalar eigenvalue `S(z)` of an elastic scattering matrix after the domain has been chosen so that

- the relevant one-channel/eigenchannel description is valid;
- universal known long-range/massless-graviton singular factors are removed or separately frozen;
- `S` is analytic and contractive in the chosen upper-half-plane/disk variable;
- boundary elastic unitarity gives `|S|=1` almost everywhere on the physical boundary interval.

This is a **scoped factorization theorem**. Four-dimensional gravity has massless exchange, inelastic channels and IR subtleties, so the gate may be applied only to a legitimately isolated/regulated hard eigenchannel.

## 2. Inner-function structure

Under the scoped assumptions, `S` is an inner function. Canonical factorization separates its dynamical data into

1. a Blaschke product determined by zeros in the analytic domain;
2. a possible singular-inner factor determined by singular boundary measure/data;
3. trivial convention/asymptotic phase factors fixed separately by normalization/growth conditions.

In elementary elastic S-matrix language, the Blaschke factors are the mathematical form of CDD-type ambiguities/resonance factors.

## 3. Finite zero data do not yield genuinely new functional hard structure

If

- the zero set is finite;
- there is no singular-inner measure;
- asymptotic phase/growth data are finite and frozen,

then `S` reduces to a finite Blaschke/CDD product: a rational/meromorphic finite-data object.

Its zeros/poles correspond to a finite resonance/state or CDD structure and must be profiled against

- ordinary resonances/mediators;
- additional particle states;
- EFT/higher-derivative completions;
- string/KK/higher-spin towers where relevant.

This may be a perfectly valid finite model, but analyticity + unitarity have not generated a new quantum-gravity origin law.

## 4. Non-rational unitary causal phase requires extra data

A genuinely non-rational inner solution requires at least one of

- an infinite zero sequence satisfying the appropriate Blaschke convergence condition;
- a nontrivial singular-inner boundary measure;
- a more complicated multi-channel/operator factorization.

Therefore the P4 origin question becomes

> what finite physical law derives the entire zero sequence / singular measure / matrix-inner structure?

If those data are simply listed, fitted or chosen after the desired amplitude is known, functional freedom has merely been repackaged.

## 5. CDD ambiguity lesson

Classic exact S-matrix bootstrap is famously able to impose

while still leaving CDD ambiguity.

Thus these consistency axioms are again **transport/selection constraints**, not generally a unique source of hard dynamics.
